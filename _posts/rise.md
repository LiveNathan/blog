# The Rise and Fall of ShowBook

The title is too dramatic, but I guess it captures a feeling, and a feeling must be what I'm after, but more on that later. 

My previous attempts at building a SaaS business have failed.

1. SubAligner - Small market
2. AV Sync - Unclear demand
3. GainGuardian - Technical challenge, no path to market
4. ShowBook - Replaced by AI

I haven't talked a lot about ShowBook directly until now. I have posted a bit about its problem area, production schedules, but I was waiting for a milestone. The milestone was going to be its first field test. I felt like after that I could confidently talk about its concrete (no speculative) value. 

With AV Sync, I kept trying to build a product by consensus. I kept doing more and more discovery interviews because business books say, "Talk to your customers about their problems. Build something to solve their problems." After talking to over 50 AV managers, it still felt like everyone needed something different. 

With ShowBook I tried a new approach: Interview 5 people, pick a single problem and move forward. This is something that I learned from Square Wave, by the way. They are often building a product or a feature for a single customer, then generalizing it for a wider customer base. 

I had one paying customer, which gave me confidence. It felt great. I could never get anyone to commit or pay with AV Sync so this felt like a big step forward. I see now that it's also quite risky to try to launch a SaaS on a single customer. 

Two big risks: 1. Single customer. 2. semi-Technical customer

Lots of people are using chatbot assistants now like chatGPT and Claude and Muse. I resist calling it AI. It's an LLM. It's just token generation with vector multiplication. Who cares.
Three weeks ago Claude released a new feature that improved its interconnection with Google Sheets. Now it can collaborate better with you. All of a sudden my one customer for ShowBook can ask Claude to read his emails and meeting transcripts, extract decisions, and update his production schedule in Google Sheets, which is exactly what we were planning to do with ShowBook. We planned a pivot, to make ShowBook the system of record (source of truth) and make it easy for Claude to read and write to it, but I knew it was the beginning of the end. There are a few strong reasons why I still think a purpose built system like ShowBook would be more reliable and robust, but it cannot compete with instant gratification. Any time Chris wants a new way to vizualize the data, he's got it. ShowBook could do it more reliably, with fewer errors, and more efficiently, and over time it could have many views of the data and even some flexible ways to create custom views, but it would be an uphill battle to get Chris or maybe anyone else to use it. And that's exactly where I don't want to be. I want to build a product that is so highly valuable that you're happy to spend the time, effort, and money to integrate it into your work. Overnight it went from being an exciting new valuable automation tool to a burden. 

It reminds me of the perenial joke: What if I could make you live 10 years longer, would you want it? Of course! All you have to do it go to the gym twice a week.

But people don't do it. I've been going to the gym weekly at the same time for about 3 years now. I never see the same people. Motivation, consistency.

What is success? 

Replace my income. Pay the bills. But also a feeling.
